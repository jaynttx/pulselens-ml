UNDERSTAND WHAT YOU BUILD

Behind the science.

Two approaches. One tiny change in reflected light.

How does a camera see your pulse?

Blood volume changes with each heartbeat, creating subtle changes in reflected light from facial skin. Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) estimates the repetition rate of these changes.

The classical path

Face detection → cheek and forehead colour traces → overlapping POS windows → detrending → spectral and autocorrelation analysis → quality gating → BPM.

The learned path

A 36 × 36 RGB face crop enters the pretrained FacePhys state-space model in Python. Its hidden state is updated with each frame. Learned SQI and spectral postprocessing support the pulse estimate; projected feature maps and recurrent states are visible live.

Experimental research panels

The breathing proxy uses 30 seconds of slow green-channel variation. Pulse-rate variability uses about 60 seconds of clean beat intervals. Video amplification highlights real temporal pixel changes, including artifacts.

What is our contribution?

Python face tracking, POS/FFT signal processing, pretrained inference, breathing and pulse variability, with a lavender web interface, model inspection, local model caching, benchmark tools, tests, and documentation.

FacePhys model weights are pretrained third-party assets; we do not claim to have trained them. The original PulseLens repository inspired this comparison.

Be honest about uncertainty

Motion, illumination, camera frame timing, skin appearance, and model training-domain differences affect estimates. Spectral concentration and learned SQI are quality measures, not probabilities of medical accuracy.
